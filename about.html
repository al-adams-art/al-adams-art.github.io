ABOUT A. L.

A. L. Adams works across visual art, found materials, altered objects, miniatures, assemblage, painting, and writing.

“What has it seen? Who held it? What secrets does it know?”

The work usually begins with attention: wear, repair, residue, scale, arrangement, and the history already present in an object or image. Materials are not neutral. They arrive with evidence.

The mediums change. The underlying interest does not. A discarded object, a painted landscape, a miniature room, or a piece of fiction can all carry traces of use, memory, contradiction, and previous lives.

The archive is intentionally direct. It records the work and the project context without manufacturing credentials or sanding everything into one marketable aesthetic.

“I find things with personality, things that still have life in them, things that don't make my brain go, ugh.”
“If I can cut it out and I can glue it down, it's going on the thing if it's interesting enough or useful.”
“And we deserve something cool and nice and that makes us smile.”
