THREE SMALL PIECES OF EVIDENCE.

Before the war, nobody particularly wanted Cooter Brown.

This arrangement suited Cooter Brown perfectly.

His father had wanted him long enough to teach him to fish, shoot, mend a fence, judge the intentions of a mule, and recognize the sound of a man approaching the place with paperwork.

He considered this civilization.

There were honest choices.

Coffee or whiskey.

Hunt today or tomorrow.

Fix the fence or accept that the cow had entered a period of geographical experimentation.

Then there were government choices.

“You told the Kentucky tax collector this was Tennessee.”

“That was a tax question.”

“You cannot change states based on the question.”

“Then why’d God put the line so close?”

Cooter objected to the word doctrine. It sounded taxable.
